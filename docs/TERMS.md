## Your part

- **Keep copies of your work.** The editor works on files on your own computer. It is not a
  backup, and neither are its snapshots or its log. Keep your documents, and the `.cowriting`
  folders beside them, in a backup of your own.
- **The AI is yours to choose.** You set up the provider or the model the editor asks. What you
  ask and your document go to that provider under your agreement with it; its terms, its prices
  and its handling of your data are between you and it. The provider of the editor is no party
  to that.
- **What an AI says can be wrong.** You decide what you make of an answer, and you answer for
  your text.
- **The disclosure is an aid.** The editor logs its own AI's answers and writes a disclosure
  from that log. Whether that disclosure is complete and meets the rules of your university,
  your journal or your employer is for you to check. The editor does not know what you did
  outside it.
- **Use within the law.** Do not use the editor to break the law or the rights of others.

## Your data

What the editor sends and to whom is in the privacy statement, which you can read in Settings
under About. In short: your documents stay on your computer; what you ask goes to the AI
provider you chose; a released version asks the provider's server at each start whether a newer
version exists, which you can switch off; figures about use are sent only if you agree to them
separately, and you can take that back at any time.

## When these terms change

A newer version of the editor can come with newer terms. It then shows them at its first start
and goes on only when you agree. If you do not, you can keep using the version you have under
the terms you agreed to.
