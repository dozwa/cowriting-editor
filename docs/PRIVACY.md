## What this is about

The editor works without sending anything about you to its maker. Only if you agree, it sends the
figures below, so that the maker can see whether and how much the editor is used. You are asked
once, both parts separately, and nothing is ticked beforehand. Saying no changes nothing about what the editor can do.

## 1. Sign of use at start (only with your consent)

Once at each start of the editor:

- a random number made on your computer when you agreed (the installation's number). It is not
  derived from your name, your computer or anything else about you;
- the editor's version;
- the kind of system (for example "macos aarch64").

## 2. Figures of a session (only with your separate consent)

Once when the editor is closed; if that fails, at the next start:

- the installation's number, version and kind of system as above;
- how long the editor was open, in minutes;
- how many requests went to an AI, by kind of provider and name of the model;
- how many documents were worked on;
- how many words were added.

## What is never sent

Your text, your questions, the AI's answers, file names, paths, the address of your provider, API
keys, your user name, your computer's name. The editor's technical log (diagnostics.log) is not
sent either; it only leaves your computer if you add it to a bug report yourself.

Chats and files from other AI tools that you keep with a statement of outside use are not sent
anywhere either, and the AI you ask in the editor is not given them. They lie in the document's
`.cowriting` folder on your computer. Whoever gets that folder from you, in a shared or synced
folder or with a submission, gets them too: keep only the conversations that belong to the work,
and mind what they say about other people.

Before a message leaves your computer, the editor encrypts it for the maker's server, with a key
built into the editor. On the way, and for a service that passes the message on to the server,
it is then unreadable; it is opened on the maker's server, checked and stored there. Encryption
does not hide that a message was sent, when, and from which internet address, and the figures
remain personal data: they carry the installation's number.

When a message arrives, the server sees the internet address it came from, as every server does.
[[whether and how long the server keeps internet addresses]]

## The check for a newer version

Apart from the figures above, and without the installation's number: a released version of the
editor asks the maker's server at each start whether a newer version exists, unless you switched
that off in Settings under About. The request names nothing: no version, no number, nothing about
your system or your documents. As with every request, the server sees the internet address it
comes from. When you start an update yourself, the new version is fetched from the project's
releases on GitHub (GitHub, Inc., USA), which then sees your internet address too.
[[legal basis for the check for a newer version, and whether and how long the server keeps internet addresses of these requests]]

## Legal basis, storage, recipients

Legal basis is your consent (Art. 6 (1) (a) GDPR; for reading the installation's number from your computer § 25 (1) TDDDG).
The figures are stored at [[hosting provider and country]] for [[storage period]] and are passed on to nobody else.

## Your rights

You can withdraw either consent at any time in Settings under About; from then on nothing is
sent, and when both are withdrawn the installation's number is deleted from your computer. Settings
shows exactly what would be sent. You have the right to information, correction, deletion,
restriction, data portability and to complain to a data protection authority. To have figures
deleted that were already sent, name the installation's number (Settings shows it while a consent stands).
